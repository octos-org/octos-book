# English Writing & De-slop Rules

Final-pass rules for English drafts. Core patterns adapted from Peter Yang's no-ai-slop skill (https://github.com/petergyang/no-ai-slop, MIT). Principle: minimum effective edit; keep the author's edge, cut only what smells like AI.

## Words to cut

Banned outright: delve, foster, leverage, utilize, facilitate, empower, streamline, robust, seamless, cutting-edge, paradigm shift, game changer, tapestry, realm, pivotal, multifaceted, meticulous, transformative, elevate, embark, harness (as a verb of enthusiasm; the noun "harness" as a technical term stays), ever-evolving.

Hedging/filler: really, just, literally, genuinely, honestly, simply, actually, truly, fundamentally, importantly, crucially.

Filler phrases: it's worth noting, at the end of the day, when it comes to, at its core, in today's world, the reality is, in order to, let's dive in.

## Patterns to cut

1. **Binary contrasts.** "It's not X. It's Y." State Y directly. Keep only when X is a live position being refuted.
2. **Throat-clearing openers.** "Here's the thing," "Let me be clear." Cut; state the point.
3. **Colon reveals.** "The best part: it learns." Rewrite as a plain sentence. Colons are for lists, labels, quotes.
4. **Importance puffery.** "Marks a pivotal moment." State the fact; let the reader judge.
5. **Weasel attribution.** "Experts agree," "studies show." Name the source or cut the claim.
6. **Superficial -ing analysis.** Trailing "highlighting/underscoring/showcasing" clauses. Replace with the concrete consequence.
7. **Synonym cycling.** If the clear word is right, repeat it. Don't rotate the agent / the assistant / the tool.
8. **Triplet addiction.** Exactly three for no reason: cut to the strongest, or use the number the content needs.
9. **Rhetorical setups and self-answered questions.** "What if I told you...", "Why does this matter? Because..." Drop the setup.
10. **Fake-profound kickers and recap endings.** Delete the mic-drop metaphor and the "In conclusion" paragraph. End on the last concrete point or a plain next action.
11. **Em dashes.** None in short copy; 1–2 in long drafts, only where they clearly beat commas or periods.
12. **Formatting slop.** No emoji in headings, no bold sprinkled for emphasis, no bullets where two sentences of prose read better.

## Conventions

- Active voice; verbs do the work ("decided," not "made a decision").
- Front-load conclusions at every level: piece, section, paragraph, sentence.
- Concrete numbers over abstractions ("cut review from 6 hours to 20 minutes").
- Technical terms (Rust, RLVR, spec, harness, agent) stay as-is; do not paraphrase them for variety.

## Keep (not slop)

- Metaphors that carry an argument, not decorate one.
- Strong opinions. Sharpen them; never sand them down to sound balanced.
- Direct quotes, verbatim.
